# Full Length Player 0.16.0-beta.7 — Patient shared stream seeking

- Shared seeks involving a network stream now wait up to 120 seconds, rather than giving up after 15 seconds. Local-only seeks retain their 15-second limit.
- Your play/pause intention survives the wait. K/Space or the shared button can choose whether to resume when both players are ready; new seeks replace the pending target.
- The shared timeline status reports which player is loading, seeking, buffering or aligning, elapsed wait time, and whether playback will resume. Fullscreen shows this status when its controls are revealed.
- Cancel wait stops automatic resume and leaves both players paused. It does not stop background downloading or unload media. It is available in the shared toolbar and fullscreen controls during the wait.
- Timeouts remain visible and suspend automatic drift correction until you press play or issue a new shared seek. Streams never unexpectedly resume after a cancelled/timed-out wait.
- Diagnostics include shared-seek state, elapsed/deadline times, resume intent, requested positions and actual player positions/durations. Replacement media and New Session clear stale restore-stage labels.

This addresses the app giving up before a slow stream is ready. It does not increase your connection speed or promise faster YouTube CDN delivery. The three-second network refill buffer and beta 5's seek-free speed changes are retained.

Please test shared backward seeks with two YouTube videos: allow a slow seek more than 15 seconds and check that both resume together. Also press pause during a wait and try Cancel wait; both should remain paused after data arrives. If trouble returns, the new diagnostic report should identify the exact wait state.

Windows verification uses real local-HTTP video/audio with a virtual deadline clock to cover long-wait recovery, pause intention, timeout and cancellation without requiring slow public servers. Real YouTube delivery still needs user testing.
